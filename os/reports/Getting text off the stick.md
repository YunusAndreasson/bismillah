# Move exact bytes by code, not OCR

The writer's exact text should leave the stick by a visible code or a courier stick, sealed to a paired phone and confirmed by check words, and OCR should handle only the paper and sources that have no code. The strongest finding is that the QR plan may be roughly 10x too cautious without changing barcode family: a conservative fast setting works out to about 26 KB/s [E] against the plan's 1 to 3 KB/s [E], and cimbar reports about 106 KB/s [M] on a low-end Android phone. The surprises sit around that core. A $23.50 dual-head FAT32 stick needs no new kernel module, a Caps Lock LED could carry a poem, and a printed manuscript with a QR appendix is both readable and exactly recoverable. The attack-research channels are mostly fun to know about, because they are slow, need extra receivers, or radiate the text. OCR cannot promise bytes for vocalised Arabic, even with a perfect reader, because the picture does not hold the codepoint order. The author's mobile-editor idea still has real value as the receiver the plan already needs, with OCR added later as a clearly unverified scanning tool. **Nothing here was tested on the real stick, a real laptop panel or a real phone**, so every fit judgement below is provisional.

Labels follow the notes. **[M]** means measured by a system's authors, and those figures are self-reported, since no independent replication was found. **[M-here]** means measured on the author's machine on 2026-10-31, either from the build tree or on rendered images with simulated degradation, never through a camera, printer or scanner ([OCR notes](../research_notes/Getting%20text%20off%20the%20stick/paper_and_ocr.md), [port notes](../research_notes/Getting%20text%20off%20the%20stick/wires_and_ports.md)). **[C]** means claimed without a usable test. **[E]** means an estimate, arithmetic or my reasoning.

## Fast codes beat the plan tenfold on paper, but no laptop figure exists

The headline rates come from generous setups. Decimen's 418.5 KB/s [M] is one record run from a 49-inch Odyssey G9 to an iPhone 17 Pro Max, and it should not be read as a laptop number ([Decimen wiki](https://github.com/bashalarmistalt/decimen-optical-transfer/wiki)). The useful figure is the conservative one: 1,465 bytes at 24 fps on one code gives a 35 KB/s ceiling, or about 26 KB/s at Decimen's 0.742 catch rate [E] ([run record](https://github.com/bashalarmistalt/decimen-optical-transfer/blob/main/benchmarks/runs/2026-08-09T04-12-41-run.json)). TXQR's author is a useful brake: about 25 KB/s [M] was a best record with a tripod and external monitor, and the same author expected 1 to 2 KB/s in most cases without fountain codes ([fountain post](https://divan.dev/posts/fountaincodes/), [earlier post](https://divan.dev/posts/animatedqr/)). So the plan's low end stays the planning floor until the bench says otherwise.

cimbar is the most mature step beyond QR. It moved 4,689,084 bytes in 44 s, about 106 KB/s [M], on a Snapdragon 625 phone with an unnamed monitor, and the rate is after zstd, so compressible text moves faster in source bytes ([PERFORMANCE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sz3/libcimbar/master/PERFORMANCE.md)). It needs only about 14 useful frames per second [E], so a 60 Hz panel is not its limit. Its cautious modes would land near 74 and 46 KB/s if they scale from mode B [E]. The weaknesses are practical: Android has F-Droid apps, iPhone has only a first-party decoder its author calls "still beta" and third-party receivers ([PR #190](https://github.com/sz3/libcimbar/pull/190)), the project calls itself experimental, and its encoder now carries an epilepsy warning ([releases](https://github.com/sz3/libcimbar/releases)).

Three stick-specific traps have no measurements yet. A 1024×1024 frame does not fit a 1366×768 panel, and cimbar's documented floor of 700×700 implies a blurring non-integer downscale [E]. On a 4K panel at 2x, a canvas must map to device pixels or every tile is resampled, a bug that shows up only as a low decode rate [E]. The stick's own QA also recorded frames shown late on software rendering ([AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md)), and cimbar's WebGL and GLES paths are an integration risk.

## Sealing matters more than speed in a reading room

Encryption is the only control that survives a camera. Privacy filters work against off-axis eyes, and any hidden channel a phone can read, a second phone can read too ([precedents notes](../research_notes/Getting%20text%20off%20the%20stick/precedents_and_sealing.md)). The strongest design [E] is to pair once at home: the phone makes an age X25519 key, the writer types its 62-character `age1…` recipient into the stick, Bech32 catches typos, and both sides compare check words. Every later export is sealed to that key with a fresh ephemeral secret, so filming the screen and keyboard gains nothing ([age spec](https://github.com/C2SP/C2SP/blob/main/age.md), [BIP-173](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin/bips/master/bip-0173.mediawiki)). Pairing by typing keeps the author's rule intact, since the stick never scans.

Short codes do not transfer from other products. Magic Wormhole's 16 bits work because an attacker gets one guess per live connection ([Magic Wormhole](https://magic-wormhole.readthedocs.io/en/latest/welcome.html)), while a filmed one-way stream can be attacked offline forever. A borrowed-phone fallback therefore needs about 16 random Base32 characters, or about 80 bits [E]. The design's costs are plain: a lost phone key opens every recording ever made, and a future quantum computer breaks X25519 recordings. Coldcard's 2026 incident shows why the ephemeral secret must come from the kernel CSPRNG and be tested under early-boot conditions: a March 2021 firmware build error left some devices on a weak software RNG, despite the air gap ([TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/the-largest-hardware-wallet-exploit-of-2026-inside-the-usd-116-million-coldcard-hack), excerpt only).

This changes one line of the plan. [os/PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) proposes "a hash shown on both sides". A bare plaintext hash on a filmable screen lets a filmer confirm a guessed text, such as a known passage, so for sealed exports the check should be keyed from the file key. AEAD success then proves every byte, including BOM, CRLF and marks, while 4 to 6 check words catch wrong-file and wrong-version mistakes. The stick can only ever say "shown", because the channel is one-way. "Confirmed" belongs to the writer after comparing words, in line with trust rule 4, and the stick must never mark a source as exported on its own evidence. Freewrite's single "synced" state is the failure to avoid ([Freewrite](https://support.getfreewrite.com/article/65-draft-not-syncing)).

## What to pursue, and what is only fun

| Route | Best evidence | 50 KB chapter | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fountain QR at a higher rate | 26 KB/s [E], plan 1 to 3 KB/s [E] | About 2 s to 50 s [E] | **Pursue first**: bench what is already being built |
| Sealing, pairing, keyed check words | Design only [E] | Adds no real time | **Pursue**: needed by every route |
| Dual-head FAT32 courier stick | 0 kernel modules [M-here] | Trivial | **Pursue** as the non-camera route |
| Laser print with QR appendix | QR exact in simulation [M-here] | Pages | **Spike**: the first real test is a phone reading a printed page |
| cimbar as a large-file mode | 106 KB/s [M] | Under 1 s [E] | **Spike after QR**: Android first |
| Record video, decode afterwards | No measurement | Unknown | **Spike**: one evening's test, removes the live camera receiver |
| HDMI capture dongle | About 30 KB/s design target [E] | About 2 s [E] | Optional, for a second computer or iPad |
| Pico "HID in, drive out" dongle | 64 KB/s ceiling [C] | About 1 s [E] | Optional and untested, charming |
| Caps Lock to camera | 15 to 45 bit/s [M] | Hours | Spike only for short pieces |

The courier stick is the most underrated route. `vfat` is already in the image at 12,324 bytes, no new module is needed [M-here], and a 64 GB dual-head stick was about $23.50 on a June 2026 listing ([price history](https://www.pricebefore.com/sandisk-ultra-dual-drive-luxe-type-c-flash-drive-64gb-p484781.html), search summary). It is the only route Apple's wording clearly allows on an iPhone, and AOSP's mount code accepts FAT on Android ([Apple guide](https://support.apple.com/en-lk/guide/iphone/external-storage-devices-iph95baac91f/16.0/ios), [AOSP vold](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/system/vold/+/refs/heads/main/model/PublicVolume.cpp)). That Apple page is iOS 16-era, and the current version timed out, so iOS 26 wording is unconfirmed. An outbox on the boot stick itself is weaker: Apple documents "a single data partition", so iPhone behaviour with ESP, verity and LUKS partitions is unknown, and a foreign computer could alter a stick that later boots [E]. Files on the courier should be sealed, with names and sizes inside the seal, because Wei et al. found single-file sanitisation "consistently fail[s]" on flash, so deletion cannot be relied on ([FAST 2011](https://static.usenix.org/event/fast11/tech/full_papers/Wei.pdf), excerpt only). The plan already lists an allow-listed second stick for backup, so this extends a known idea.

Paper is the most interesting route for this writer. A 300-word vocalised page is about 1.7 KB deflated [E], and a 100,000-word Arabic book is about 190 v40-L codes [E], so capacity is not the limit: readback is. Optar's roughly 200 KB per A4 needs a 1200 dpi flatbed and has no current camera readout ([Optar](https://ronja.twibright.com/optar/)). Low-density QR has the only credible phone path, and paperbackup's QR codes survived seven print-scan generations and a soak in dirty water [M] ([paperbackup](https://github.com/intra2net/paperbackup)). A clean printout with a final QR appendix is the best layout [E], since a whole-page v40 code is about 47 mm square at conservative settings [E], too big for a footer. I found no per-page precedent in a manuscript. SecureDrop's guidance also prefers analog export ([journalist guide](https://docs.securedrop.org/en/stable/journalist.html), excerpt only), though the printed text alone never carries exact bytes. `usblp` is 12,148 bytes [M-here] and raster output keeps the app's own shaping, but no printer path has been checked against the stick's design. Thermal paper is a handover copy at most: vendors claim six months to two years for standard rolls [C] ([POSPaper](https://www.pospaper.com/blogs/news/does-thermal-paper-fade)), a QR failed one crude dot-spread simulation [M-here], and U+06DB did not visibly appear in the test render [M-here].

HDMI and the Pico dongle are clever optional extras. An $11 to $19 capture dongle (2022 prices) shows frames to a computer with nothing added to the stick, but Desjardins' hobby tests recovered a roughly 2 MB file and then received only 1.55 MB of a 54 MB one ([Desjardins](https://patrickdesjardins.com/blog/how-to-transfer-files-between-computers-using-HDMI-Part-7-pagination)), the 550 KB/s figure is theoretical [C] ([hdmiFileTransfer](https://github.com/yesyesno8/hdmiFileTransfer)), and developers report iPhones find no external camera ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/775883), search summary). Whether cage mirrors to the port was not checked. The Pico design adds no module, because `CONFIG_HIDRAW=y` and `usbhid` are present [M-here], and it could appear as a small drive to the phone, but that half is untested and firmware provenance matters. Typing the text out through it is exact only for what the receiver's layout can produce, so it cannot carry vocalised Arabic without an ASCII armour and a decoder.

The rest of the physical channels are stories rather than features. The best stock-phone figures still take about 51 minutes for 50 KB (three keyboard LEDs at 130 bit/s [M]) to about 1.85 hours (POWER-SUPPLaY at 60 bit/s [M]) before overhead, and many laptops have one LED, which gives about 92 minutes after compression and error correction [E] ([CTRL-ALT-LED](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.05851), [POWER-SUPPLaY](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00395), [channel notes](../research_notes/Getting%20text%20off%20the%20stick/physical_side_channels.md)). One spike is tempting: rolling-shutter reception has reached over 1,200 b/s [C] with an LED ([abstract](https://archive.cps-vo.org/node/38847)), and nobody has published it against a Caps Lock LED. A tiny LED may cover too few sensor rows [E].

USBee (80 B/s [M] to a $30 RTL-SDR, using the stick itself as the antenna), RAMBO, COVID-bit, BitJabber and TEMPEST-LoRa all work against the central promise, because the stick would be deliberately radiating the writer's text ([USBee](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.08397), [TEMPEST-LoRa](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.21069)). "No radio driver" is a lawyer's distinction, so these are fun to know and wrong to ship. System Bus Radio playing a tune on an AM radio is the best anecdote, with no data rate at all ([project](https://github.com/fulldecent/system-bus-radio)). MAGNETO puts a phone on the palm rest with basic permissions and takes about 22 hours at 5 bit/s within 3 cm of a desktop ([MAGNETO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.02317)). Fansmitter takes weeks, and BitWhisper takes years and a second PC ([BitWhisper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.07919)).

Invisible page codes also fall short. High-rate systems need 120 Hz panels, and unobtrusive 60 Hz methods run at about 1 to 5 kbps [M], well below the plan's 8 to 24 kbps [E], with a custom receiver ([DeepLight](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05092)). StegaStamp holds 56 usable bits per image, about 7 characters, which is a fingerprint at best ([StegaStamp](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Tancik_StegaStamp_Invisible_Hyperlinks_in_Physical_Photographs_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf)). The ambient light sensor is an estimated 15 to 25 B/s at best [E], and Android-only. Laptop gadget mode, xHCI debug and Prolific bridge cables are dead ends: most PCs are host-only, Debian leaves `USB_XHCI_DBGCAP` off, and `plusb` is networking, which the plan forbids [M-here].

## The OCR mobile editor: a real editor, but not an exact path

**The idea.** The phone app is a real editor rather than a receiver. The writer scans the laptop screen or a printed page, the text lands in an editable document, and the writer corrects it there. The appeal is genuine: no stick-side code at all, no frames, and it works on any page in any room. The evidence says it cannot deliver the writer's bytes, for three stacked reasons.

**First, accuracy on marks is far too low.** The phone systems a writer would reach for do not list Arabic: Apple's iOS 26 Live Text list has no Arabic entry, and ML Kit v2 lists none ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/ios/feature-availability/), [Google](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/text-recognition/v2/languages)). Apple's Vision API may differ, and only an on-device call can confirm it ([Vision](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnrecognizetextrequest/supportedrecognitionlanguages().md)). The most careful open study gets 89.44% on inherited characters, which include vocalisation, with its generalised model and 79.67 to 85.18% with typeface-specific ones [M], on scans of ordinary print ([OpenITI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10943)). At 89%, a page with 1,000 marks has about 100 wrong [E]. Character error rates on mixed Arabic run from 0.13 for Gemini-2.0-Flash to 0.54 for Tesseract [M] ([KITAB-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14949v2)), and QARI v0.2 reaches 0.061 on a synthetic diacritic-rich set [M] ([QARI-OCR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02295)). Even the best reader on a font-matched task falls short: 99% per character gives about a 10⁻¹³ chance of an exact 3,000-character page, and 99.99% would be needed for about 74% [E]. Quran-specific results of 98.90% and 99% [M] are self-reported on easy conditions, and one uses a random split over a single font, so test words are not independent ([TechScience](https://www.techscience.com/cmc/v68n2/42154/html), [Mushaf study](https://www.academia.edu/61367037/Quranic_Optical_Text_Recognition_Using_Deep_Learning_Models)).

The local runs set a floor, and only a floor, since Tesseract is the weakest serious recogniser here.

| Text and font, Tesseract at 300 dpi | Condition | CER | Marks kept | Lines exact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uthmani, Amiri Quran | Clean | 77.7% | 18 of 427 | 0 of 16 |
| Uthmani, Amiri | Clean | 78.8% | 31 of 427 | 0 of 16 |
| Uthmani, IBM Plex Sans Arabic | Clean | 34.9% | 242 of 427 | 0 of 16 |
| Uthmani, IBM Plex Sans Arabic | Light degrade | 27.3% | 304 of 427 | 0 of 16 |
| English, Source Serif, 330 characters | Clean and degraded | 0% | n/a | 5 of 5 |
| English, iA Writer Mono | Clean | 0.61% | n/a | 3 of 5 |

These are [M-here] on rendered images. Marks kept is a count and flatters the result, since a mark can be present and wrong. The degraded image beating the clean one shows how noisy so small a sample is. No study of photographed screens for Arabic was found at all, and moiré arises when screen dots approach the camera's sampling size ([arXiv 2111.14075](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.14075)), so a screen is likely harder than paper [E].

**IJMES fails quietly.** Tesseract's `eng` model turned `al-Fātiḥa, Qurʾān, ḥadīth, ʿilm, ṣalāh, ḍalāl` into `al-Fatiha, Qur'an, hadith, 'ilm, salah, dalal`, and `Āl ʿImrān` into `Al Tmran` [M-here]. All macrons and underdots were lost, and the output reads as plausible English. Tesseract's Latin script model was not tested and might keep them. Plain English is the bright spot, with clean byte-exact output in one font on a small sample.

**Second, the picture does not hold the bytes.** This limit survives any future recogniser. Shadda+fatḥa and fatḥa+shadda are expected to render identically, as are precomposed and combining forms and some Yeh variants, and ʿ can match ‘ in a given font, so a perfect reader still has to guess [E, from Unicode and shaping, not a rendering test]. Models also normalise toward their training data: Kraken's practice deliberately omits vocalisation, and "the low penalty reflects omission of tashkeel, not faithful preservation" ([Farazi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22366)). Hallucination was 62.4% of QARI's attributed errors on manuscripts [M], and print would be lower but not zero. In scripture a plausible error is the worst kind.

**Marking uncertain spots helps, but cannot be trusted alone.** The notes contain no evidence that any recogniser's confidence is calibrated on Arabic marks, so this is design reasoning [E]. The dangerous errors are the ones with nothing to flag: an omitted mark has no low-confidence glyph, and a hallucinated word looks confident. So the app should flag by structure as well as confidence, such as stacked marks, known lookalike classes, and lines whose mark count falls below its neighbours, and it should keep the source image beside each line. It must never present an unflagged line as correct. On a vocalised page at the measured rates, that could mean flagging about 100 or more spots, and the correction itself is unmeasured. No note covers the time needed to correct marks on a phone, or how many errors a writer misses.

**Can a hash from the stick confirm the corrected document?** Yes, but only rarely, and it says only yes or no. The stick can show a SHA-256 of the file bytes, as 6 words plus 16 hex characters, and the app can compare it. A match proves exact bytes. In the OCR path a bare hash leaks little extra, because the plaintext is already on the screen or page [E], which also means this path can never be sealed.

The limit is invisible order. A check for this report hashed the same word with both mark orders: `مَّن` with fatḥa then shadda and with shadda then fatḥa gave `2a28b5c4…` and `8c577286…`. Both orders are expected to look the same, so a writer correcting by eye cannot see which one fails [E]. The repo's own files show this is common rather than exotic. In `tests/fixtures/quran/tanzil-uthmani.txt`, all 53 multi-mark runs containing shadda put it first, and NFC would change 55 of 67 multi-mark runs. `docs/specimen.md` mixes both orders in one file (2 shadda-first, 9 not), so no single convention reproduces it [M-here, codepoint counts only]. Marks typed on a phone keyboard add a further unknown order.

A hash also gives no location. Two additions would make it more useful, both untested [E]. Per-line checks, as in paperbackup's 6-character line checksums, would locate the failing lines, at the price of clutter on a quiet page. A second "reading check", computed over a temporary NFC copy, would merge mark-order differences, and it follows ADR-0004's rule that normalisation happens only in a temporary copy. Its honest label would be "reads the same, bytes not confirmed". It would not merge Yeh variants or ʿ against ‘, which stayed distinct under NFC in the same check. Those remain failures a writer cannot see by eye. Parity data to repair OCR is dominated: it needs a lossless channel, in practice a QR code, and at 10% mark errors it approaches the size of the compressed page. As the notes put it, if the screen can show a code, show the data.

**Governance.** A mobile editor conflicts with [ADR-0009](../../docs/decisions/0009-desktop-only.md), which says the product "will never be a web or mobile app", so it needs a new ADR superseding that sentence. The recogniser falls under [ADR-0004](../../docs/decisions/0004-spelling-and-grammar.md): it must run offline, only read, and leave every change to the writer. Diacritic restorers such as Mishkal or Shakkala insert marks the writer may not have written and must stay out, whatever their error rates ([Fadel et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.01965)). Whether a vision-language model counts as a reader under ADR-0004 needs the author's ruling. The best scores in the benchmarks come largely from hosted services, which ADR-0003's offline rule excludes on the writer's text [E], and no note shows the stronger open models running on a phone. Trust rule 3 also needs restating on a phone, where text widgets, autocorrect and keyboards can change bytes, and any received original must be stored as opaque bytes. Finally, the phone is networked, and "once text is on a networked phone, every promise ends" should be said plainly to the writer.

One consequence is easy to miss. QR is output only, so edits made on the phone never return to the stick by that route. A mobile editor therefore creates a fork, and any stick hash stops applying after the first deliberate edit. The new ADR has to say which copy is the master.

**The hybrid the evidence supports.** The same simulated degrade separates the two paths cleanly: QR decoded byte-exact in every test down to 2 pixels per module, including v40-L with 2,953 bytes, while Arabic OCR got 0 of 16 lines [M-here]. That points to one app with two doors and two visible states. Text received by code arrives as a frozen original marked "Exact, N bytes", confirmed by AEAD and check words, and a first edit creates a new revision marked "Edited from exact". Text scanned by camera arrives marked "Read by camera, unverified", with uncertain spots shown, and it can never earn the exact label short of a full hash match. OCR then serves paper, books and other people's sources that have no code, plus plain English convenience and search. Printed manuscripts join both doors: the phone reads the QR appendix for bytes, and scans the page only when a code is destroyed. The stick never scans, so the author's decision holds.

This also solves a problem the notes keep finding. The companion app is the weak part of this category, as Pomera's thin listing hints (3.7 of 5 from just 3 ratings, too small to generalise) ([pomera Link](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id371963393)). iPhone also lacks a confirmed cimbar app, and pairing requires an app holding a key. So build in order: receiver, then reader, then editor, then OCR. Each step is useful alone, and the OCR step can be dropped without harm.

## Corrections and gaps to carry forward

Several figures in circulation are wrong or need care. BRIGHTNESS modulates pixels, not the backlight, and aIR-Jumper used security-camera IR, not laptop face-recognition emitters ([BRIGHTNESS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.01078), [aIR-Jumper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.05742)). USBee's own rate is 80 B/s, so LED-it-GO's 4,800 bit/s table entry should be ignored ([LED-it-GO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.06715)). ODINI's "40 bit/s at 100 to 150 cm" contradicts its own text, which limits that rate to 5 to 20 cm, and it used a lab sensor ([ODINI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.02700)). TEMPEST-LoRa's "21.6 bps" is a typo for kbps. TXQR's "25kbps" looks like a units error for KB/s. COVID-bit's prose claims 500 bit/s at 0% BER, which its table contradicts ([COVID-bit](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.03520)). RAMBO's abstract and conclusion disagree ([RAMBO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02292)), as do POWER-SUPPLaY's range claims and its tables. Uber-in-Light is INFOCOM 2016, not MobiSys. The PGP word list is two 256-word lists, not 2,048 words ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGP_word_list)). DeepLight's re-normalised figures for rivals are a competitor's estimates, and COBRA's low file goodput was measured by RDCode's authors. The notes also differ on cimbar: its README says mobile decoding is Android-only, while newer sources show a beta web decoder, and the newer sources are preferred here.

The gaps decide the plan. Nothing here measures any code on a 13 to 15-inch 60 Hz panel, on 1366×768, under Mesa software rendering, or handheld on the stick. Matte against glossy panels and the cost of PWM have no measurements. No phone has read a dense printed QR page in this research, and no phone OCR benchmark of photographed vocalised Arabic exists. A related housekeeping point: `crates/bismillah-print` names ADR-0021, and `docs/decisions/` has no such file yet.

## Conclusion

The research started by asking how far past animated QR the channel could be pushed, and the answer turned out to be less about channels than about what the writer can honestly be told. Exotic physics adds stories, while the promise rests on three plain things: a sealed payload, a check the writer performs, and a status that says "shown" until then. The same logic settles OCR. A scanner can be a lovely part of a phone editor as long as its output says it was read by a camera, because the failure that matters is a false claim of exactness on scripture.

The next steps are cheap and should come before any new ADR. Spend an evening with the real phone and the real panel: run QR at rising frame rates through a stock-camera video test, read a laser-printed QR page, and put the courier stick into both phones. Then scan one vocalised page and one IJMES page with the best on-device recogniser available and time the correction against ground truth. My suggested test for the editor's OCR door is simple: if correcting a flagged vocalised page takes longer than retyping it, or if the writer misses marks at any meaningful rate, OCR stays English and paper-search only, and the mobile editor stands or falls on being a good home for exact text.
