# How will we think?
## Attention
I am old enough to remember mechanical typewriters. Without the luxury to edit words or rearrange the text, you were forced to concentrate. One slip, and the only options were to start over or use Tipp-Ex to cover the mistake. That sounds like a constraint, but the attention it required forced you to mentally connect with the text in a way not necessary when you know that it is easy to change.

Fast forward into the digital era and everything became fluid. This was a technological leap forward, but for human capabilities, in the form of attention – it was a loss.
## Thinking
With large language models we are no longer writing, we are prompting, asking for texts to be generated that we skim through, and if it needs to be shared, we often share it as if it were our own writing and thoughts.

Prompting is not thinking, writing is.

When you have a machine in front of you that can deliver anything you want, instantly, it is not in the nature of the human to not use it. It is the law of least restraint.
## Memory
A book is at the same time a preserver of memories and their destroyer. A preserver as human history was recorded in them, and a destroyer as humans no longer had to memorise their own history.

The book won for memory but we still remember. The word processor won for attention but we still attend. How will we think?
