# The laptop scene's moments

Made on 2026-10-29 with Codex's image generation (`codex exec -i <reference>`), at the
author's request, over four rounds, two variants of most and the stronger kept. The
laptop scene shows what the app is for one idea to a moment, as an Apple commercial
would: her own words (`words.jpg`), Zuhd (`zuhd.jpg`), Arabic and English
(`arabic.jpg`), privacy (`tape.jpg`), and any laptop becoming hers (`anywhere.jpg`).
A sixth, writing offline at sunrise in the desert, was made and then left out (the
author, 2026-10-29); its prompt stays below.

What the rounds taught, for the next picture:

- **One idea a viewer gets in a second.** Metaphors failed: a dictionary for spelling, her
  son reading for Arabic, a phone in a dark drawer for "nothing else". What works is one
  plain act of hers (the tape peeled off the camera) or the app itself on her screen.
- **Physically right.** She uses the laptop, so its screen faces her: the camera is
  behind or beside her, never in front. Two rounds had her typing on a screen turned to
  the camera, which no one can do; it is the first thing that makes a picture false.
- **Authentic, not stock** (the author, after Nolan): real places, real light, worn
  things; no posing at the camera.
- **Screens at the app's own size.** A screen shown larger looked false; where the
  writing must be read, the camera comes close instead, as in Zuhd.
- **Readable on a phone against the light page:** the film's grade, lightened
  (`scripts/laptop.py`, GRADE).

The references were the film's character still of her (the director project's
`lookbook/writer-02.png`) and, for the night scenes, the film's frame of her at her
laptop (`media/stills/03-her-screen.jpg`). The screens were generated blank; the app's
real screens are laid on by `scripts/laptop.py`, which finds each blank page, or takes
its corners measured by hand where its glow is uneven or the light around it is as
bright.

Check every new picture at full size for the geometry, logos, lettering (no invented
print may be readable), the stick (in the laptop's left side, only its body showing),
and the modesty rules.
