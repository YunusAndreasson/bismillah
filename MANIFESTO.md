# How will we think?
## Attention
I am old enough to remember mechanical type writers. That was before digital word processors, even if the concept of _word processing_ existed in your mind as you had to internalise the sentence before typing it. Without the luxury to edit words or rearrange the text, you were forced to concentrate. One slip, and the only options were to start over or use Tipp-Ex to cover the mistake. That sounds like a constraint, but the attention it required forced you to mentally connect with the text in a way not necessary when you know that it is easy to change. Self discipline can not set this scene, only constraints.

Fast forward into the digital era and everything became fluid. Text could now be edited, moved and restructured when you no longer had to keep the paragraph in your own memory, since whatever you wrote down was temporary and could easily be altered. The constraint to preprocess sentences was gone as they could easily be changed afterwards.

This was a technological leap forward, but for human capabilities, in the form of attention – it was a loss.
## Thinking
But whatever development we saw during the early digital years, you were still in charge of your thinking. The spelling and grammar hints offered suggestions on how the language could be corrected and structured but that was a matter of semantic structure, not of essence.

That is different today.

With large language models we are no longer writing, we are prompting, asking for texts to be generated that we skim through and if it needs to be shared, we often share it as it was our own writing and thoughts. Convenient, effective and no constraints.

The volume of text that can be produced is virtually infinite but just as the technological advancement of word processor, when attention was lost, we are now loosing something even more valuable, our ability to think.

Prompting is not thinking, writing is.

At first, it is not going to be noticeable as it happens gradually, like the frog being boiled, but it will happen. We can try to fool ourselves to believe that we can incorporate processes to keep us in charge as we interact with the models, but when you have a machine in front of you that can deliver anything you want, instantly, it is not in the nature of the human to not use it. It is the law of least restraint.

To lose the ability to think is hard to imagine and the only thing that serves as a precedence is the ability to memorise. And that was taken away by another technology, the book.
## Memory
A book is at the same time a preserver of memories and their destroyer. A preserver as the human history was recorded in them and a destroyer as the humans no longer had to memorise her own history. In modern times, this might seem trivial, since our time frame is to short to have seen this happen but hearing about masters of Islamic scholarship, especially in the field of memorising hadith when memorising hundreds of thousands of prophetic traditions was not unheard of. Today this would have been seen as superhuman or having a photographic memory. But when stories were told orally, and the only way to preserve them was through the human memory, that kept the memory strong.
## Evolve or dissolve
As for memory, we still preserve fragments, maybe not as detailed but we still remember by pointers to a book, a person we can ask or the Internet through a search engine. For attention we learnt, or at least we believe we learnt, to multitask.

Is it going to be the same when we outsource our thinking? Do we even have to think or will thinking be diminished to a leisure when all industrialized cognitive work is done by the more effective language models?

With all technology, resistance is futile, as it will progress, so at which layer can the humans interact in relation to the LLMs. The book won for memory but we still remember. The word processor won for attention but we still attend. How will we think?
